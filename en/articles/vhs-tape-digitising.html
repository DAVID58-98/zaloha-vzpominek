Digitising VHS tapes – how to handle the transfer of classic video cassettes

Found a box of old video cassettes at home? Weddings, family celebrations, children's first steps, holidays – these memories are priceless, but on VHS they will not last for ever. Physical media age, and the quality of the recording drops every year. Digitising VHS is now the only reliable way to save these moments for future generations.

In this guide from Záloha vzpomínek we look in detail at why transferring VHS to USB matters so much, what threatens your tapes and how professional video tape digitisingbrings your memories safely into the 21st century.

The clock is ticking: why your VHS is losing quality

Many people assume that a cassette safely stored in a cupboard is safe. The opposite is true. The magnetic tape inside a VHS cassette is highly prone to decay, and time is working against you.

  • Demagnetisation: over time the magnetic layer weakens, which shows as loss of colour, a faded picture, more static and worse sound.
  • Physical damage: tape can become brittle or sticky, and mould can appear if the cassettes were stored somewhere damp.
  • Mechanical wear: every playback wears the tape slightly – so, ironically, watching the video is what destroys it.
  • No equipment left: as we mention in our frequently asked questions, VHS players stopped being made long ago. Finding a working, decent player gets harder every year.

Every day you put video tape digitising off is a day you risk losing your memories for good. That is why at Záloha vzpomínek we focus on rescuing these recordings before it is too late.

⚠️

An important warning: if the tape has snapped, never repair it with sticky tape. The adhesive spreads inside the video mechanism and can destroy both the cassette and the player. Better not to play a damaged cassette at all, and have it dealt with professionally.

How professional VHS digitising works

You may have thought about digitising VHS at home. Cheap USB capture devices exist, but their quality is often poor – the result is a jittery picture, badly synchronised sound and generally worse quality than the tape itself can offer. At Záloha vzpomínek we therefore take a professional approach that guarantees the best possible quality:

  1. Visual inspection: every cassette is checked first. If the tape has snapped or is dirty, we offer additional services such as splicing and mould cleaning (see our price list).
  2. Top-end equipment: for the VHS transfer itself we use professional Panasonic NV-HS1000 video recorders with TBC (Time Base Correction), which digitally stabilises a jittery picture.
  3. Signal conversion: the analogue signal passes through an external Canopus ADVC 300 A/D converter, also with TBC, which is what keeps the sound perfectly in step with the picture.
  4. Basic treatment included: we crop the black edges, reduce noise, normalise the audio and balance the colours.
  5. Saving to a medium: the finished digital file goes onto the medium you choose. The most popular is VHS to USB or VHS to a flash drive, which makes playback easy on a PC, a laptop or a modern television.

The complete process is also set out in the How it works section.

What format you get the video in

As standard we convert to MP4 with the H.264 codec. It is a universal format that plays on virtually anything – a computer, a smart TV, a phone or a tablet. If you need a different format, just tell us in advance through the contact form.

The advantages of going digital

Why go through a VHS transferat all? The advantages are considerable:

  • A permanent backup: digital files do not age, fade or lose quality – your backup of memories will be safe for generations.
  • Easy sharing: email the video to family or upload it to the cloud.
  • Available instantly: play it whenever you like on a computer, a phone or a smart TV.
  • Better organised: instead of a box full of bulky cassettes, all your memories sit tidily on one USB drive.
  • Room to edit: want to cut your own film from the old footage? With digital files that is straightforward.
